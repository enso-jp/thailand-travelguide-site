## What happens on 6 November

- **The occasion:** the **Royal Kathin Ceremony** at Wat Arun, where His Majesty the King presents robes to monks at the end of Buddhist Lent. This year it also marks Her Majesty Queen Suthida's 48th birthday anniversary.
- **The fleet:** **52 royal barges** rowed by **2,200 Royal Thai Navy** crew, in five columns. The formation is about **1,280 metres long** and 90 metres wide.
- **The barges to look for:** **Suphannahong**, the golden swan that carries Their Majesties, plus Anantanakkharat, Narai Song Suban King Rama IX and Anekkachatphutchong.
- **Start time:** Suphannahong leaves **Wasukri Pier at about 15:00**, passes under Rama VIII Bridge and heads 3.9 km south to Wat Arun.
- **The route:** Phra Sumen Fort, Somdet Phra Pinklao Bridge, Siriraj Hospital, the Grand Palace and the Royal Thai Navy Auditorium, then Wat Arun.
- **Why it matters:** the procession only sails for major royal ceremonies, so it does not happen every year.

## Where to watch for free

- **Who can watch:** anyone. TAT invites the public and visitors to watch from **both banks between Krung Thon Bridge and Wat Arun**, with no ticket and no fee.
- **Old town bank:** Santichaiprakan Park by Phra Sumen Fort, Tha Maharaj and Tha Chang all face the route.
- **Thonburi bank:** the riverside park by Siriraj Hospital and the pier area at Wat Rakhang.
- **Wider or closer:** upriver near Rama VIII Bridge you see more of the fleet at once. The Wat Arun end has the famous backdrop, and the biggest crowds.
- **Reserved seats:** the Navy's free seats at the Royal Thai Navy Club and the Navy Convention Hall were handed out by phone and in person in September. That registration has closed.

## River boats stop from noon

- **The closed stretch:** from **12:00 until the procession ends**, no boats may pass between **Krung Thon Bridge and Somdet Phra Pinklao Bridge**, or on Khlong Bangkok Noi from its mouth to Wat Suwannaram.
- **Chao Phraya Express and electric boats:** they turn back short of the zone. Board at **Phayap Pier or further north**, or at **Ratchawong Pier or further south**.
- **Cross-river ferries:** on the August rehearsal days, the ferries from Tha Chang, Tha Maharaj and Tha Phra Chan, and the Wat Pho to Wat Arun crossing, stopped from noon. Expect the same on 6 November, and pick your bank before you set off.
- **Take the MRT instead:** on the Blue Line, **Sanam Chai** is the old town stop near Wat Pho. **Itsaraphap** is on the Thonburi side, about 1 km on foot from Wat Arun.

## Before you go

- **Arrive early:** be on the bank by about 13:00. The best spots fill well before the barges move, and there is little shade.
- **Dress politely:** this is a royal and Buddhist ceremony. Cover shoulders and knees, as you would at a temple.
- **Grand Palace:** we found no posted closure for 6 November. Check the official Grand Palace schedule the day before, and keep sightseeing for the morning.
- **Wat Arun:** it hosts the ceremony itself, so save your visit for another day.
- **Roads:** expect closures around the old town in the afternoon. Bangkok's traffic information line is **1197**, and the TAT Contact Centre is **1672**.

## Back on the river from 13 November

- **Vijit Chao Phraya 2026:** TAT's light festival runs **13 November to 13 December** at 13 riverside sites between Rama III Bridge and Rama VIII Bridge, including Wat Arun and Memorial Bridge. It is free to watch from the banks.
- **Not yet published:** the nightly times and the dates for the drone and fireworks shows.
- **Loy Krathong:** the full moon festival falls on Tuesday 24 November. Dates and Chiang Mai rules are in our [Loy Krathong 2026 story](/stories/loy-krathong-yi-peng-2026-dates).
